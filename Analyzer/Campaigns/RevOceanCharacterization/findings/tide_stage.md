# tide_stage: exact forms of the Tide comb and of the voice filter

Packet `tide_stage`, wave 2. Model: `tide_stage.py`. Scores, round-value tests and checks:
`tide_structural_data/tide_stage.json`, regenerated by `fit_tide_stage.py` (2 min 12 s with the
captures cached). Numbers marked "exploration" come from scratch scripts in
`Analyzer/Results/RevOceanCharacterization/work/tide_stage/` and are not regenerated.

Tags: MEASURED (read off captures), FITTED (a constant, residual given), INFERRED (reasoned, not
tested), OPEN.

## 0. Operating point and the two kinds of null

- Mix 100 % in every capture; Macro 100 % is the main point (9 instances), 90, 75, 60, 50, 35, 25,
  15, 10, 5, 3 and 1 % give the laws against Macro. Decay 0.5 s, Size 100 %, baseline otherwise;
  host at 44.1 kHz (where the internal rate is seen without converters) and at 48 kHz.
- Stimulus: unit impulses of 0.5, alternating between the inputs, 0.8 to 1.0 s apart.
- A null is `20 log10(rms(model - reference) / rms(reference))` per impulse and output, nothing
  fitted but the phase of the voice (its value, or its value and its rate).
- **Model null**: `tide_stage.FirstPass` (the Macro 0 network of `first_order_model`, unchanged,
  behind the Tide stage) against a capture on the first-pass window (raw 1200 to 2500 at 48 kHz,
  1080 to 2255 at 44.1 kHz; shifted by the comb delay at Macro 100 %). The network model itself
  stands at -80 dB, so this null cannot go below that.
- **Relation null** (44.1 kHz host): the stimulus is passed through this module's input stage and
  rendered at Macro 0; that capture, with the resting low-pass divided out and this module's voice
  applied, is compared with the capture of the plain stimulus at Macro above 0. Both sides are
  captures of the reference, the network is not modelled, and the null is that of the Tide stage
  alone.
- "Open" cases: the window stands above -30 dB re the voice at rest. A closed voice leaves only
  the tail of the previous impulse.

## 1. Result

```
in ─┬──────────────────── cos(90° m) ──┐
    └─ comb ───────────── sin(90° m) ──(+)── Macro 0 network ── voice (in place of the 20 kHz, Q 1 low-pass) ── out
all of it at 44.1 kHz between the two rate converters, at every host rate;  m = Macro, 0 to 1
```

**Comb**, one per input channel, the same in every instance and at every Macro, Size and Decay:

```
v[c]    = LP(HP(read(line, D[c])))            HP, LP: bilinear one-pole sections at 30 Hz and 20 kHz
line[c] = in[c] - 0.4 v[c]                    both sections are inside the feedback loop
read    : first-order all-pass, y = x[c-i-1] + a (x[c-i] - y[c-1]), i = floor(D), d = D - i in [0, 1), a = (1-d)/(1+d)
D[c]    = 431.2075 + 236.0360 tri((c - 85.7) / (200 s x 44100) + channel/4)   samples at 44.1 kHz, single precision
```

`c` counts internal samples from the start of processing; `tri` is a unit triangle, 0 at 0 and
rising; the right input is a quarter period ahead. In time: 9.77795 +- 5.35229 ms, 4.42566 to
15.13024 ms.

**Voice** (measured for voice A: lines 1 to 8 of an output). A two-pole low-pass with the transfer
function of the bilinear ("audio-EQ") low-pass at 44.1 kHz, realised as a trapezoidal
state-variable filter, with a gain behind it. Cut-off, Q and gain are computed once per block of
44 samples (1 ms) and held; blocks start where the internal sample count is a multiple of 44.
With `phi` the phase in cycles, `a = 0.88 m`, `d = min(1, a / 0.05)` and
`E(x) = (1 - e^(-3x)) / (1 - e^(-3))`:

```
cut-off   fc = 20000 - 19990 E(a) E(phi mod 1)                Hz        (lowest value 463.86 Hz at Macro 100 %)
gain      g  = (1 - d) + d sqrt(2) sin^2(pi phi)
Q target  Qt = 1 + d (K(fc) - 1);  K piecewise linear in Hz through
          (1 kHz, 7.01)  (3 kHz, 10 - 7.91 E(m))  (5 kHz, 7 - 5.64 E(m))  (15 kHz, the same)  (20 kHz, 1),
          below 1 kHz a straight line of slope 3.645e-3 per Hz (5.056 at 463.86 Hz)
Q         Qt smoothed per block by a one-pole with a time constant of 10 blocks
```

At Macro 0 this is the fixed filter of the Macro 0 model (20 kHz, Q 1, gain 1); the voice does not
sit behind that low-pass, it is that low-pass.

## 3. The comb

Every statement is a model null at Macro 50 %, 44.1 kHz, median of 74 cases, with one element
changed (`combTestsModelMacro50MedianDb`); as published: -80.05 dB.

- **Rate.** The comb runs inside, at 44.1 kHz, behind the input converter. At a 48 kHz host the
  published model gives -79.75 dB; the same comb run at the host rate in front of the converter
  -31.18 dB. MEASURED.
- **Read rule.** First-order all-pass with the fraction in [0, 1). Linear interpolation -26.4 dB,
  four-point Lagrange -34.0 dB, all-pass with the fraction in [0.618, 1.618) -35.4 dB (each at the
  best of five delay offsets). MEASURED. The all-pass coefficient goes up to 1 when the delay
  nears a whole number from above; impulses with fractions of 0.004 to 0.008 at their exit still
  null at -66 to -74 dB with this rule and at -35 to -45 dB with any other (exploration).
- **Feedback: -0.4, not -8 dB.** -8 dB (-0.398107) gives -62.3 dB, the earlier -0.3986 gives
  -64.6 dB. The earlier value was the ratio of the second to the first exit, which includes one
  more pass through the two filters (the high-pass alone has a gain of 0.99787). FITTED; a scan
  is symmetric about -0.4 (0.5 dB lost at -0.3995 and at -0.4005; exploration).
- **Filters of the delayed path.** A bilinear one-pole high-pass at 30 Hz (35 Hz: -47.6 dB,
  25 Hz: -46.6 dB) and a bilinear one-pole low-pass at 20 kHz (19 kHz: -32.9, 21 kHz: -33.0 dB).
  The low-pass is new. It was found as a free first-order section in the delayed path whose fit
  returned a zero at z = -1.003, a pole at z = -0.744 (20.004 kHz) and a DC gain of 0.99784, the
  gain 1/(1+k) of the bilinear high-pass (exploration). Its low-frequency delay of 0.073 samples
  is most of the "0.10 samples longer" of the verification. FITTED.
- **Both filters sit inside the loop.** Behind the loop instead: -37.6 dB. MEASURED. This is why
  the wet has no DC at Macro 100 %: every exit passes the high-pass once more.
- **Delay law.** Per-impulse delays of the all-pass read (204 cases, plug-in time 11 to 158 s,
  both inputs, both flanks, `delayLaw`): the published law leaves 1.8e-5 samples rms (largest
  5.7e-5); a free fit returns mid 431.20749, swing 236.03601, origin 85.68 samples, period
  200.0000 s, rms 1.6e-5. A delay 0.01 samples longer: -44.9 dB; period 200.005 s: -47.5 dB;
  origin 44 samples (the reported latency): -51.9 dB, 88 samples: -75.1 dB. FITTED. The delay is
  the one in force when the sample is read, it is smooth from sample to sample (a staircase of 44
  samples would leave 1.4e-3 samples rms), and the 1.8e-5 samples are the size of single-precision
  rounding at 512 to 1024 (step 6.1e-5).
- **The delay is a single-precision number.** When the delay falls through a whole number the
  read changes its taps, and a transient that left the comb shortly before sees it. 44 impulses
  were timed to leave the comb 100 samples before such a crossing, at crossings where the exact
  law lies within 0.75 single-precision steps below the whole number (`combDelayCrossings`). Each
  is a two-valued test: the null is near -70 dB when the model changes taps at the reference's
  sample and near -37 dB when it is one sample off. The exact (double-precision) law puts 31 of
  44 right; `fl32(mid) + fl32(swing) x fl32(tri)` puts 41 right. MEASURED. The same count as a
  function of the origin peaks at 85.70 to 85.72 samples (32, 41, 33 of 44 at 85.6, 85.7, 85.8;
  exploration), an independent reading of the origin. The exact arithmetic is OPEN: three of the
  44 events and one impulse of the main 44.1 kHz train (63.170 s, delay 605.014, null -41 dB at
  every Macro value) are still on the wrong sample. Other single-precision chains tried in pure
  arithmetic (rounding of the double sum, a single-precision phase by division or by product, a
  unipolar triangle, delays in milliseconds or seconds) match 33 to 41 of 44 at best
  (exploration).
- **Constants without a round form.** 431.2075 and 236.0360 samples are 9.77795 and 5.35229 ms;
  the extremes are 4.42566 and 15.13024 ms. None is round in samples, milliseconds or hertz. The
  origin of 85.7 samples (1.943 ms) is neither one nor two blocks of 44. OPEN. At a 48 kHz host
  the same law reads 469.34 +- 256.91 host samples, the numbers of packet `tide`.
- **Crossfade.** The gain of the delayed path relative to `tan(90° m)` times the undelayed path,
  fitted per impulse together with the phase (`crossfade`): median 1.00000 or 1.00001 at Macro
  1, 3, 5, 10, 25, 50, 75 and 90 %, quartiles within 1e-4 at 1 % and within 2e-5 from 5 % up. The
  absolute gain (relative gain offset fitted with the phase) is within 2e-5 of
  `sqrt(2) sin^2(pi phi)` times the crossfade in the middle of a cycle at Macro 10, 25, 50 and
  100 % (exploration). So the mix is `cos / sin(90° m)` over the whole range, also below 10 %.
  MEASURED. The 0.1 to 0.3° deficit of the earlier packets was the missing low-pass.
- **Size and Decay.** Section 2: relation nulls of -95 to -106 dB at Size 62 and 157 % and at
  Decay 1 s, with the comb and voice constants of Size 100 %, Decay 0.5 s. MEASURED.

## 4. The voice

Round-value tests are medians of the relation null at Macro 100 % over 147 open cases, one
element changed (`voiceTestsRelationMacro100MedianDb`); as published: -93.9 dB.

**Transfer function.** At a 44.1 kHz host a free biquad (five numbers) per impulse on the
undelayed stretch at Macro 5 to 75 % has the numerator `1 + 1.67425 z^-1 + 0.74829 z^-2` (spread
5e-5 to 3e-4): the denominator of the bilinear low-pass at 20 kHz with Q 1 at 44.1 kHz
(1.674254, 0.748297). Three numbers (gain, cut-off, Q of a bilinear low-pass over that
numerator) null as well as five (-57.5 against -57.5 dB with constant parameters; exploration).
So relative to Macro 0 the voice is `g LP(fc, Q) / LP(20 kHz, 1)`: it replaces the fixed
low-pass. The verification's numerator (1.4410, 0.7639; "19.3 kHz, Q 2.15") was this one seen
through the 48 kHz converters. MEASURED.

**Per-block update.** A steady 997 Hz tone at Macro 100 % comes out with a family of lines at
997 Hz + k x 1002.22 to 1002.28 Hz, that is every 44.000 to 44.002 samples (`tone`). Their level
is -76 to -80 dB re the tone for k = 1 and falls about 6 dB per doubling of k (a sawtooth); it
does not depend on the tone's level (0.05, 0.2, 0.45), so it is not distortion; at Macro 50 and
10 % it is -89 dB, at Macro 0 there is no such family (-103 dB, the skirt of the tone). In the
relation: parameters held for 44 samples -93.9 dB, parameters following the phase per sample
-76.7 dB; blocks one sample later or earlier -86.2 / -85.6 dB, 22 samples later -71.5 dB. The
blocks do not follow the host block (64, 512, 2048 score alike) and start at multiples of 44 of
the internal count at a 48 kHz host as well (model null -79.7 dB at offset 0 against -71.1 dB at
22 and -75.3 dB without hold; exploration). The gain is held too (gain per sample with the rest
held: -74.6 dB, exploration). MEASURED.

**Recursion.** Trapezoidal state-variable low-pass, gain behind the filter. Direct form I with
the same coefficients: -84.9 dB (9 dB worse); gain in front of the filter: -83.2 dB. The
transposed direct form II scores between the two and the direct form II far worse below 3 kHz
(exploration). FITTED: the state-variable form is the best of four, not shown to be the only one.

**Cut-off law.** `fc = 20000 - 19990 E(0.88 m) E(phi)`. With the phase read from the gain, the
cut-off of 127 to 145 impulses per Macro value follows `f_low + (f_high - f_low)(1 - E_k(phi))`
with f_high = 19999.7 to 20000.1 Hz and k = 2.99996 to 3.00033 at Macro 75, 50, 25 and 10 %, rms
0.0014 to 0.013 % (exploration). The lowest cut-off is 1867.2, 4582.4, 9835.8 and 15118.8 Hz
there and 463.86 Hz at Macro 100 %; `20000 - 19990 E(0.88 m)` gives 1867.23, 4582.44, 9835.81,
15118.77 and 463.86. Tests: law starting at 19.3 kHz -26.3 dB, bend 2.94 -33.6 dB (the values of
packet `tide`, which were readings of this law through a 48 kHz bilinear form); law ending at 20
or 0 Hz instead of 10 Hz: -60.0 dB each; amount 0.87 instead of 0.88: -46.6 dB. FITTED. What is
measured is `f_low(m) = 20000 - 21037.4 (1 - e^(-2.64 m))`; reading it as "the curve E at 0.88 m,
ending at 10 Hz" is INFERRED, supported by the depth slope below.

**Gain law.** `sqrt(2) sin^2(pi phi)` at full depth: a relative gain offset fitted next to phase
and rate is within 2e-5 for phases 0.2 to 0.8 and within 2e-4 at the ends of a cycle, at Macro
10, 25, 50 and 100 % (exploration). Gain top 1.40: -40.3 dB. MEASURED.

**Q law.** Piecewise linear in the cut-off in Hz, with knots at 1, 3, 5 and 15 kHz and the end
at 20 kHz:

| | 1 kHz | 3 kHz | 5 to 15 kHz | 20 kHz |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| K at Macro 100 % | 7.01 | 2.09 | 1.36 | 1 |
| K against Macro | 7.01 | 10 - 7.91 E(m) | 7 - 5.64 E(m) | 1 |

- Plateau: medians of 1.36000, 1.46339, 1.69010, 2.04564, 2.38888, 3.14154, 3.86822 at Macro
  100, 90, 75, 60, 50, 35, 25 % against 1.36000, 1.46339, 1.69009, 2.04562, 2.38888, 3.14155,
  3.86823 from the formula (exploration, static form). Plateau 1.40: -53.2 dB.
- 3 kHz: 2.0900 at Macro 100 % (2.089 and 2.091 are 6 to 7 dB worse on the cases between 1 and
  5 kHz), 2.23496, 2.55273, 3.05154 measured at 90, 75, 60 % against 2.23500, 2.55294, 3.05157
  (exploration). 1.5 times the plateau (2.04, packet `tide`): -69.8 dB.
- 1 kHz: 7.010 +- 0.001 at Macro 100, 90 and 75 % alike (model null of the impulses on the 1 to
  3 kHz slope against the knot: -61.8, -67.5, -73.9, -76.7, -73.4, -67.5 dB at 7.000, 7.005,
  7.008, 7.010, 7.012, 7.015 at Macro 100 %; exploration). 7.00: -86.9 dB over all cases, -63 dB
  on the cases it concerns.
- Below 1 kHz: slope 3.645e-3 per Hz +- 0.003e-3 (-88.8 dB against -80.8 and -77.9 dB at 3.640
  and 3.650e-3 on 32 cases; exploration), measured down to 464 Hz and at Macro 100 % only. The
  module writes it as a knot (0 Hz, 3.365); where the law's own knot lies is OPEN.
- Above 15 kHz: a straight line to Q = 1 at 20 kHz (ends measured 0.994 to 1.002 at five Macro
  values; exploration). This is the "Q roll-off above 13 kHz" of packet `tide` and the "behaviour
  above 12 kHz" asked for: no other element is needed there.
- Knot frequencies: the lines of adjacent segments meet at 3000.2 +- 0.3 Hz and 5000.1 +- 0.5 Hz
  once the lag below is in (2994 and 4992 Hz without it; exploration).

**Q lags the cut-off.** Q is not the law's value at the current cut-off but follows it 9.5 blocks
late. A one-pole per block with coefficient `1 - e^(-1/10)`: -93.9 dB; time constants of 9 or 11
blocks: -82.2 / -82.8 dB; no smoothing: -63.4 dB. The same smoothing applied to 1/Q, or a pure
delay of 9.5 ms, score within 0.6 dB of it, also on the 55 cases next to a knot (exploration).
FITTED for the lag; the one-pole form is INFERRED.

**Below full depth (Macro under 5.68 %).** `d = 20 x 0.88 m` scales the gain window towards 1
and the Q law towards 1; the cut-off law is not scaled. Model nulls: -80.9 dB at Macro 5 %,
-80.6 dB at 3 %, -80.3 dB at 1 %. Depth slope (`depthSlopeModelMacro3MedianDb`): -80.6 dB at
17.60, -70.6 dB at 17.59 and at 17.61. FITTED: 17.600 +- 0.003, that is `0.88 / 0.05`. The
earlier packets' failure below Macro 10 % was the missing Q blend and the 48 kHz bilinear form;
no second structure is needed.

**The wrap.** When the phase passes a whole number the cut-off returns from its lowest value to
20 kHz within one block, the Q target drops to 1 and Q follows through its smoothing. Below
Macro 5.68 % the gain is not zero there. One window that holds the wrap (Macro 3 %, 55.4 s)
nulls at -75.8 dB once the block of the wrap is searched, against -12.7 dB when it is not; the
fit script therefore offers one start phase per block (`wrap_starts`). MEASURED on a handful of
windows, not systematically.

## 5. What failed, and limits

- First attempts: a static filter per impulse stops at -44 dB at Macro 100 % (the sweep inside a
  window); per-sample parameters stop at -76 dB (the hold); double-precision delay leaves a few
  impulses at -41 dB. Each was the lead to the next element.
- A lag of Q was at first fitted as 9.0 to 9.3 ms with per-sample parameters; with the hold it
  is 9.5 blocks.
- The apparent "lower Q the faster the sweep" below 1 kHz was the gain sitting behind the
  filter, not a Q effect.
- The exact single-precision arithmetic of the comb delay (41 of 44, section 3). Consequence:
  about one transient in ten that leaves the comb within some 150 samples before a downward
  whole-number crossing is modelled at -37 to -45 dB instead of -70 dB. The 44.1 kHz captures
  share one such impulse; it sets their energy-weighted nulls (-68 to -73 dB at Macro 25 to
  100 %) while the medians stay at -78 to -81 dB.
- Form of the Q smoothing (one-pole on Q, on 1/Q, or a delay), section 4.
- The Q law below 1 kHz beyond its slope, and at Macro below 75 % for the 1 kHz knot (the
  cut-off does not get there).
- No round form for the delay's middle, swing and origin.
- Voice B (lines 9 and later) was not measured: its window always holds voice A content as
  well. That it follows the same forms at `phi + 1/2` is the earlier packets' result. INFERRED
  here.
- The phase generator is outside this packet. Fitted phase and rate per impulse and output of
  every scored capture are in the data file (`modelScores[].records`) for whoever takes it up.
  Mean fitted rates: 0.0570, 0.0547, 0.0531, 0.0526, 0.0504, 0.0517 cycles/s at Macro 100, 75,
  50, 25, 10, 5 % (48 kHz, one instance each; one 100 s curve is good to about 0.001).
- Not tested: hosts at 88.2 and 96 kHz, a Macro change while playing, levels above 0.5.
- What limits the relation at -94 dB is not known; the captures are single-precision.
- Capture budget: about 1.07 GB instead of 1 GB. The comb-driven Macro 0 captures were rendered
  three times because their stimulus contains the comb constants, which changed twice (origin,
  single precision).
