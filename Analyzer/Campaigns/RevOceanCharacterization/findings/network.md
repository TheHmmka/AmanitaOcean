# network: the campaign's model of the network at Macro 0

Packet `network`, wave 2, 7 October 2026: judge and integrator of the packets
`network_a` and `network_b`. Model: `network_model.py` with the core loop
`network_model.c`. Constants and evidence: `tide_structural_data/network.json`,
regenerated by `fit_network.py` (98 s once the captures are cached; three
consecutive runs wrote byte-identical files). Reference: Arturia Rev OCEAN
1.0.0.5848, Tide mode, neutral baseline.

Tags: MEASURED (read off captures), FITTED (a constant, residual given),
INFERRED (reasoned, not tested), OPEN, REFUTED. A null is
`20 log10(rms(model - reference) / rms(reference))` with no gain, delay or
polarity fitted.

## 0. Operating point, and the owner's listening point

- **Mix is 100 % and Macro (Tide) is 0 % in every capture of this packet.** The
  model is the foundation, not the sound at Tide 100 %.
- Nothing here was measured above Macro 0. That the same network runs between
  the Tide comb and the Tide voices is the result of packets `first_order`,
  `laws` and `tide` (first arrivals only, -47 to -52 dB). Whether the loop
  (matrix, kernel, tap law) is unchanged at Macro 100 % is OPEN; it cannot be
  nulled there because the reference does not repeat. For that layer the model
  exposes the outputs of single lines and of groups of lines in front of the
  output filter (section 4.4).
- Other limits: Brightness 0 %, Width 100 %, input filter open, input peaks at
  or below 0.5 (the level-dependent stage of packet `io` is not in the model).

## 1. Result

- **One structure.** `network_a` and `network_b` describe the same network.
  Their matrices, orders of operation, output weights and width law are
  identical; where they differ, `network_a` holds a fitted approximation of
  what `network_b` holds as a round-valued form (section 3). Every difference
  inside the network was decided on captures neither author used, all in
  favour of `network_b`'s forms. Two things are neither author's: the plug-in
  evaluates the display law of Size in single precision (section 3.3), and the
  converters are those of packet `converters`, which extends the model to
  every host rate that packet covers.
- **Locked holdout**, `score_network.py network_model.py`:
  **worstOverallDb -114.07, meanOverallDb -116.79**, worst stretch -92.62 dB.
  Run three times (after the first complete fit, after the regeneration of
  the data file with more evidence, after a clean-up of the code that changes
  no number), identical every time. Nothing was fitted or chosen on it.
- **Fresh captures** (programme seed 60606, 16 settings neither author nor the
  holdout used, 44.1, 48, 88.2 and 96 kHz; nothing fitted on them):
  -111.81 to -118.47 dB overall, mean -115.57 dB, worst stretch -99.44 dB.
- **100 s tails**: -104.8 dB overall at Decay 20 s and -109.7 dB at Decay 40 s,
  Size 140 %; -82.9 and -88.9 dB in the last ten seconds, where the reference
  is 539 and 313 dB below full scale (section 6).
- The model has **one fitted number**, the input gain of line 1
  (0.24397766). A free fit of 25 constants on the fresh captures and two
  100 s tails returns the round values within 2.3e-5 relative (section 7).
- A render of 14 s takes 0.2 s at a 44.1 kHz host and 0.6 s at 48 kHz.

## 4. The model

Everything runs at 44.1 kHz at every host rate. `m` counts internal samples
from the first sample the plug-in processes, warm-up included. Group `g` is the
output channel (0 left, 1 right); lines `n` = 1 to 16. `fl()` is rounding to
single precision.

### 4.1 Signal flow

```
host input -> input converter (converters.py; none at a 44.1 kHz host) -> u_g[m]

constants at Decay T (seconds) and Size s (Size / 100):
  N[g][n]    = floor(P[g][n] * s + 0.5)                      whole length
  att[g][n]  = 10^(-3 * P[g][n] * s / (44100 * T))           unrounded length
  own[n]     = B[n] * (1 + w[n]) / 2,   cross[n] = B[n] * (1 - w[n]) / 2
  tap[n]     = output weight at Decay T (4.3)
  theta[g][n] starts at fl(11.25 * (2 * (n - 1) + g)) rad, single precision
  inc        = fl(2 pi * 0.6 / 44100) = 8.5485517e-5
  D          = fl(fl(0.88f * 0.001f) * 44100f) = 38.808002 samples

per internal sample m:
  x_g[m] = E(u_g)[m - 44]                 E: two peaking sections, per channel; then 44 samples
  for each group g:
    for each line n:
      t = theta[g][n]; if (t >= fl(2 pi)) t = fl(t - fl(2 pi)); theta[g][n] = fl(t + inc)
      L = fl( fl(N[g][n]) + fl(D * sine(t)) )                           single precision, three roundings
      i = floor(L);  f = L - i
      r = (1 - f) * line[g][n][m - i] + f * line[g][n][m - i - 1]       linear interpolation
      a[n] = att[g][n] * r                                              output tap and feedback signal
    y_g[m] = sum_n tap[n] * a[n]
    for each line j:
      v = (1/4) * sum_n H[16 - j][n - 1] * a[n]                         H[r][c] = (-1)^popcount(r & c)
      line[g][j][m] = own[j] * x_g[m] + cross[j] * x_other[m] + K_j(v)  K: two shelving sections, own state per line
  out_g[m] = LP(y_g)[m]                   low-pass, per channel

out_g -> output converter (converters.py); at a 44.1 kHz host: 44 samples
```

- The wrap test subtracts 2 pi once per sample, so the start phases (up to
  348.75 rad) come into range during the first 55 samples of processing.
- `sine(t)` is `fl(sin(t - k (fl32(pi/2) - pi/2)))` with `k` the nearest whole
  number to `t / (pi/2)`: each quadrant lags the true sine by another
  4.37e-8 rad. A product needs only a sine; the rule matters for a null.
- A read uses samples written at least 270 samples earlier, so the order of
  the lines inside a sample does not matter; the input and the fed-back signal
  are written in the sample in which they are computed (no extra sample).
- The output tap is taken behind the attenuation and in front of the kernel:
  a first pass does not see the kernel.
- Matrix and kernel commute; a fast Walsh-Hadamard transform in natural order
  whose output `r` is written to line `16 - r` gives `v`.
- The model computes signals in double precision; only oscillators and line
  lengths are single precision.

### 4.3 Laws over the lines

`S_c(x) = sign(x) (e^(c |x|) - 1) / (e^c - 1)` on [-1, 1] (the vendor's Exp map
on each half).

```
w[n]   = 0.272                         n = 1 .. 6
       = 1 - (n - 7) * 0.728 / 9       n = 7 .. 16
B[n]   = B_1 * ( (1 + 0.236)/2 - (1 - 0.236)/2 * S_3.36((n - 8.5) / 7.5) )       B_1 = 0.24397766 (fitted)
p      = (min(T, 6 s) - 0.5 s) / 5.5 s
t1     = 0.46 + (0.228 - 0.46) * p
t16    = 0.336 + (0.428 - 0.336) * (e^(-2.6 p) - 1) / (e^(-2.6) - 1)
tap[n] = (t1 + 1)/2 + (1 - t1)/2 * S_4((n - 5) / 4)             n = 1 .. 9
       = (1 + t16)/2 + (t16 - 1)/2 * S_2((n - 12.5) / 3.5)      n = 9 .. 16      (tap[9] = 1)
```

### 4.4 The module

```
render(stimulus, sample_rate, warmup_seconds, decay_seconds, size_percent)            raw host output, the scorer's contract
first, internal = network_input(stimulus, sample_rate, warmup_seconds)                input converter
taps = line_outputs(internal, first, decay, size, groups=None, weighted=True)         (frames, 2, groups) in front of LP
host = network_output(taps.sum(axis=2), first, sample_rate, start, stop)              LP and output converter
```

- `line_outputs` returns `tap[n] a[n]` summed over each group of lines
  (`groups=None`: the 16 lines one by one; `weighted=False`: `a[n]`). Index `k`
  belongs to internal sample `first + 44 + k`. It takes Decay and Size as the
  plug-in works with them (`host_value`); `render` takes display values.
- MEASURED (`evidence.lineGroups`): the 16 line outputs, summed and passed
  through `network_output`, are `render` (-308 dB) and null the reference at
  -118.06 dB; lines 1 to 8 carry 67 % of the tap energy of that capture
  (Decay 1.8 s, Size 73 %).
- `run_core` accepts line lengths computed elsewhere and `oscillator_phases`
  returns the accumulators; `fit_network.py` uses both for section 3.2.

What the table shows:

1. **The null does not depend on the level.** The reference is followed from
   -25 to -539 dBFS; the relative error is set by the number of passes behind
   a sample, not by how far the response has fallen. MEASURED.
2. **Up to about 10 s (150 passes) the error is incoherent**: -100 to -118 dB,
   falling by about 3 dB per doubling of time. INFERRED (from `network_b`'s
   measurement, not repeated here): single arrivals whose line length is one
   single-precision step off, 5 in 5568 with the model's sine. One such
   arrival early in a response is what a stretch with a single impulse shows
   (holdout: -92.62 dB at Size 157 %, where a step of a long line is 2.4e-4 to
   9.8e-4 samples); sustained material averages them (-112 to -119 dB).
3. **Beyond that a coherent term takes over: the reference falls behind the
   model in time**, linearly. MEASURED: the least-squares delay of the
   reference against the model grows by 2.8e-6 samples per second at Decay
   20 s, Size 100 % (2.6e-4 samples after 95 s) and by 1.4e-6 samples per
   second at Decay 40 s, Size 140 %. Per pass that is 1.8e-7 and 1.3e-7
   samples: a property of a pass through the loop.
   Seen per band between 1 and 4 kHz, where the late response lives, the phase
   lag per pass is 2.9e-8, 4.7e-8, 5.8e-8 and 6.9e-8 rad at 1.25, 1.75, 2.5 and
   3.5 kHz, roughly proportional to frequency (exploration `x07`, `x08`). With
   one gain and one delay per stretch removed the null stays at -95 dB out to
   100 s (rows "removed").
4. **The lag is reproducible and can be absorbed by the kernel.** A free fit
   of the loop kernel removes it: `network_b`'s free fit, made on its own
   captures of at most 30 s, takes the last ten seconds of the two tails from
   -82.9 and -88.9 dB to -93.0 and -92.8 dB, and this packet's free fit takes
   the 48 kHz tail, which it was not fitted on, from -82.8 to -95.7 dB. The
   two free fits agree on the low shelf frequency (1220.9960 and 1220.9958 Hz
   for 1221) and differ in the three numbers of the high shelf, which act
   alike between 1 and 4 kHz. A constant 1.5e-7 to 2.0e-7 samples added to
   every line length does almost as well (-92.3 to -94.9 dB; exploration
   `x09`). Which of the two it is, is OPEN (section 8).
5. **The gain agrees to 1.2e-8 per pass.** MEASURED: the least-squares gain of
   the reference against the model stays within 6e-7 over 100 s at Decay 20 s
   and drifts to +1.2e-5 at Decay 40 s, Size 140 %. INFERRED: that is the size
   of the single-precision rounding of the 16 attenuation gains (up to 3e-8
   each per pass) and of the parameter values; five ways of rounding the gains
   move the drift by up to 7e-6 at 100 s and none removes it in both tails
   (exploration `x05`).
6. **The reference's output stops falling at about 1e-36** (-722 dBFS).
   MEASURED at Decay 0.65 s (`evidence.outputFloor`): -96.6 dB at -641 dBFS
   (10.0 s), -42.5 dB at -688 dBFS, 0 dB from 11 s on, where the capture holds
   a slowly moving -1.0e-36 (left) and +3.4e-37 (right). The model goes on
   falling. Its cause is OPEN and of no consequence.

## 8. What is still unexplained

1. **A lag of 1.4e-7 to 1.8e-7 samples per pass** of the reference against the
   model with round constants (section 6, items 3 and 4): -83 dB after 100 s at
   Decay 20 s, below -100 dB in the first 14 s. Two descriptions fit: kernel
   parameters 1e-8 to 1.5e-5 away from the round values (low shelf at
   1220.996 Hz in two independent fits), or a mean line length longer by that
   amount, which is 4e-9 in the mean of the reference's sine times the depth
   (0.07 of a single-precision step of the sine). Tested and REFUTED as the
   cause: cookbook coefficients evaluated or stored in single precision in
   nine variants (-47 to -73 dB after 100 s, far worse than the round
   double-precision kernel; exploration `x08`), so the reference's kernel is
   not a pair of single-precision direct-form sections.
2. **The reference's sine.** Its argument reduction is measured; its last place
   is not. 5 of 5568 line lengths (network_b) differ by one single-precision
   step, which sets the -100 to -118 dB floor of the first seconds.
3. **`B_1` = 0.24397766**: fitted, 6.7e-7 below the one closed form found.
4. **Why these numbers**: 1221 Hz, 12840 Hz, Q 0.484 and Q 0.26 of the kernel,
   200 and 1750 Hz with Q 0.4 of the equaliser, 0.236 and 3.36 of the input
   curve, 0.46 / 0.228 / 0.336 / 0.428 and the knee at 6 s of the taps.
   INFERRED: kernel and equaliser are the neutral point of Brightness, the
   width law that of Width; only Brightness 0 % and Width 100 % were measured.
5. **The arithmetic of the signal path**: per-line gains agree to 1.2e-8 per
   pass (section 6, item 5); which single-precision expression produces the
   attenuation is not established. The output floor at 1e-36 (item 6).
6. **The parameter arithmetic** is INFERRED from five captures at one tie
   (Size 50 %); Decay has no such test.
7. **Macro above 0**: whether loop, taps and gains are unchanged (section 0).
8. Not covered: input peaks above 0.5, Freeze, host rates below 44.1 kHz (the
   Analyzer refuses them).
