# first_order_b: the first pass, read in the frequency domain

Packet `first_order_b`, 6 October 2026. Model: `first_order_b.py`. Fit and checks:
`fit_first_order_b.py`. Constants and check results: `tide_structural_data/first_order_b.npz`
(arrays, plus a JSON string `facts`).

Tags: MEASURED (read off captures), FITTED (model constant, residual given), INFERRED (reasoned, not
tested), OPEN.

## Settings: what this packet did and did not look at

- **Mix 100 %**: yes, every capture. MEASURED: with impulses at least 1 s apart nothing leaves the
  plug-in before raw sample 1150 (-139 dB re the scored window at Macro 0, -121 dB at Macro 50 %,
  -117 dB at Macro 100 %); there is no dry path.
- **Tide (Macro) 0 %**: the model, the fit and the holdout score are for Macro 0, as the packet and the
  locked holdout prescribe. **They are not a model of Tide 100 %.** What Tide 50 % and 100 % do to the
  first pass was probed on 120 responses and is in the section "Tide 50 % and 100 %" below: the same
  line pattern is still there, but it arrives through an extra delay that grows with time and with a
  gain that swells and differs between instances. The Macro 0 model does not predict it.
- Size 100 %, Decay 0.5 s, Width 100 %, 48 kHz, block 512, 10 s warm-up unless stated.

## The model

Times: `n` host sample of the impulse, counted from the first stimulus sample; `k` core index,
`k = t * 147/160` for host time `t`. `W = 441000` core samples of warm-up.

```
x[k]   = P(k - n*147/160 - 46)                                 pre-kernel P, zero phase, +-16 samples wide
d_l[k] = D_l + 38.808 * sin(phi_l[k + W])                      delay of line l when sample k is read
r_l[k] = (1 - f) * x[k - N] + f * x[k - N - 1]                 N = floor(d_l[k]), f = d_l[k] - N
c_b[k] = sum_l G[a,b,l] * r_l[k] + second passes               a input channel, b output channel = network
y_b[k] = sum_q Q[q] * c_b[k - q]                               post-kernel Q, peak at q = 141
out_b(t) = band-limited interpolation of y_b at k = t*147/160

phi_l: single precision,  phi += float32(2*pi*0.6/44100) every core sample;  if phi >= 2*pi: phi -= 2*pi
phi_l at the first processed sample = 2*pi * (0.0003351 - 0.2095074 * j),  j = 0..15 over L1, R1, L2, R2, ...

second pass a->b of one network:  r_ab[k] = read_b(loop * r_a)[k],  gain S[a,b];  loop = 12-tap kernel
```

Line lengths `D_l` in core samples (44.1 kHz):

| line | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| left network | 1031 | 1097 | 1187 | 1289 | 1423 | 1583 | 1783 | 2027 |
| right network | 1039 | 1109 | 1193 | 1301 | 1429 | 1597 | 1787 | 2039 |
| G same side / decay gain, line 7 = 1 | 0.6081 | 0.7182 | 0.7065 | 0.6747 | 0.6468 | 0.6312 | 1 | 1.0240 |
| G cross / G same side | 0.57233 | 0.57233 | 0.57233 | 0.57233 | 0.57233 | 0.57233 | 0.00000 | 0.04215 |

`decay gain = 10^(-3 D / (44100 * 0.5 s))`. Mid-band (3 to 4 kHz) gain of the left first passes:
0.0456, 0.0528, 0.0505, 0.0467, 0.0429, 0.0398, 0.0593, 0.0562.

## Evidence

### 1. The core runs at 44.1 kHz; the lines are read with linear interpolation there

This is the answer to README item 7.

- MEASURED. Transfer function of the isolated arrival L7 for 980 impulse times. Its high-frequency loss
  relative to 1 kHz spans 0 to -5.37 dB at 14 kHz, 0 to -9.15 dB at 17 kHz, 0 to -13.7 dB at 19 kHz.
  A linear interpolator at 48 kHz can lose at most 4.3 dB at 14 kHz and 7.1 dB at 17 kHz; one at
  44.1 kHz loses 5.31, 9.05 and 13.4 dB. The implied rate from the four frequencies is 43.8 to 44.4 kHz.
- MEASURED. The loss follows `|1 - f + f e^{-j 2 pi F / 44100}|` with `f` the fractional part of the
  arrival's low-frequency delay expressed in 44.1 kHz samples: correlation 0.9996 (6 kHz) to 0.99993
  (17 kHz), residual 0.01 to 0.04 dB rms. Against the fraction at 48 kHz the correlation is 0.005.
- Consequences. Fixed sample-rate converters sit on both sides (the pre-ringing, the roll-off above
  18.5 kHz, most of the latency); all grid times are multiples of 160 samples and see the core lattice
  at one position; the 2.235 ms shift between 44.1 and 48 kHz hosts of README item 8 is the pair of
  converters (INFERRED, not captured here).
- MEASURED. Captures with warm-up 10.001 s and 10.0005 s (48 and 24 samples off a lattice period) are
  predicted at -69.99 and -69.95 dB when the lattice is anchored at the first processed sample. The
  null changes by at most 1.4 dB between quarters of the lattice position (-69.8 to -71.2 dB).

### 2. Sixteen lines of prime length, two independent networks

- FITTED. With the lengths left free, all sixteen land on an integer plus a common 95 (a convention of
  the first fit): fourteen within 0.0004 core samples, R5 within 0.0007, L8 within 0.002 (it overlaps
  the second pass, which that fit did not have). MEASURED: the second pass through line 1 arrives at
  `2 D_1 + fixed delay` with a modulation depth of 77.57 core samples (two reads of the same line);
  with the first pass at `D_1 + fixed delay` this gives `D_1 = 1030.9`, fixed delay 187.1.
- MEASURED. With that offset all sixteen lengths are prime; no other offset from -110 to -80 makes
  even the eight left lengths prime. The fixed delay outside the lines is 187 core samples (4.2404 ms,
  203.54 host samples), the peak of pre-kernel * post-kernel.
- MEASURED. Each output channel hears eight lines. The other input reaches the same lines (the gain
  ratios in the table are identical in both directions to five digits). Second passes from a line of
  one network into a line of the other are absent: gain below 5e-5 of the expected size for R1->L1 and
  L1->R1, against 0.179 inside a network.

### 3. The oscillators

- FITTED. One line, delay per impulse against read time: a pure sinusoid leaves 0.0197 core samples
  rms with a second harmonic of 0.027 samples; a single-precision accumulator stepped by
  `float32(2 pi 0.6 / 44100)` and wrapped at 2 pi leaves 0.0001 samples rms and returns
  `D = 1878.00000` (1783 + 95) and depth 38.80800 = 0.88 ms. The accumulator's rounding makes the
  rate depend on the binade of the phase; its mean rate is 0.5998781 Hz, which is the 0.59988 Hz of
  the reconnaissance.
- FITTED, round values tested (clean validation, start phases refitted for each candidate): accumulator
  -70.06 dB; ideal sinusoid at 0.6 Hz -2.75 dB; ideal sinusoid at 0.5998781 Hz -27.81 dB; depth 38.800
  samples -40.92 dB, 38.814 samples (the reconnaissance's 0.8803 ms) -43.41 dB.
- FITTED. One oscillator per line. The sixteen start phases form an arithmetic progression in the order
  L1, R1, L2, R2, ..., L8, R8: step -0.2095074 cycles (-1.316374 rad), residual 1.2e-6 cycles rms.
  The step has no closed form that I could find (22/105, pi/15 and 1 - sqrt(5/8) are off by 1.6e-5,
  6.8e-5 and 7.7e-5 cycles per step, ten or more times the residual). OPEN.
- MEASURED. The time origin is the first processed sample, warm-up included. Captures with warm-up
  5, 9, 10.5 and 20 s null at -70.35, -71.05, -70.88, -70.81 dB when the impulse time is counted from
  there, and at -15.9, +3.0, +3.0, -10.2 dB when it is counted from the stimulus. Block boundaries play
  no part (README item 2: bit-identical for block sizes 64 to 960).
- The delay is the one of the sample being read out (`d_l[k]` multiplies the reading of sample `k`).
  INFERRED from the fit; evaluating it at the impulse time instead leaves a second harmonic of 0.047
  samples in the single-line study.
- Convention. The start phase of L1 is 0.0003351 cycles with 46 core samples of fixed delay in front
  of the lines. It would be exactly 0 with 70.6 samples in front; only the sum 187 is measured.

### 4. The fixed kernel and where it sits

- MEASURED. The composite kernel (pre * post, core rate): peak at 187; magnitude re 3 to 4 kHz
  +0.8 dB below 300 Hz, 0 dB at 1 to 4 kHz, +0.43 dB at 12 kHz, +0.95 dB at 17 kHz, -2.4 dB at 19 kHz,
  -13.9 dB at 20 kHz, -46.6 dB at 21 kHz; pre-ringing over about 30 core samples.
- FITTED. Its excess group delay from 3 to 20.3 kHz is that of one bilinear low-pass biquad (audio-EQ
  form) with corner 19998.6 Hz and Q 1.0006, residual 0.02 samples rms. Round values 20 kHz and Q 1 are
  used. After dividing it out, two linear-phase converters remain: together -0.1 dB at 18 kHz, -3.6 dB
  at 19 kHz, -13.9 dB at 20 kHz, -35.9 dB at 21 kHz (-6 dB each near 19.85 kHz = 0.9 x 22.05 kHz).
- MEASURED by Doppler. A kernel in front of a modulated line is read out time-compressed, one behind
  it is not, so the placement is observable. Clean validation, post-kernel, gains and start phases
  refitted for each placement:

  | pre-kernel in front of the lines | null |
  | --- | --- |
  | unit impulse (everything behind the lines) | -50.91 dB |
  | whole composite magnitude, zero phase (only phase behind) | -50.06 dB |
  | half of the composite magnitude, zero phase | -65.33 dB |
  | sqrt(composite / low-pass biquad), zero phase (stored) | **-70.06 dB** |

  So: a linear-phase converter in front with half of the two converters' magnitude, the 20 kHz
  low-pass and everything dispersive behind the lines. The velocity regression per frequency says the
  same: the part in front has flat group delay (within 0.05 samples) up to 19.5 kHz.
- The 36 samples of pre-ringing are the two linear-phase converters; the post-ringing is the biquad.
- Not parametrised. The post-kernel is 300 free taps and six exponential tails (time constants 250
  to 8000 core samples). The low-frequency part (+0.8 dB below 300 Hz, group delay 2.5 samples shorter
  at 500 Hz than at 5 kHz, a slow negative tail) was not turned into named filters. OPEN.

### 5. Gains

- MEASURED. Same-side gains of the two networks differ by exactly the decay gain of their length
  difference: left over right per line 1.00251, 1.00377, 1.00189, 1.00376, 1.00189, 1.00438, 1.00123,
  1.00375; `10^(-3 dD / 22050)` gives 1.00251, 1.00377, 1.00188, 1.00377, 1.00188, 1.00440, 1.00125,
  1.00377. A line read is therefore scaled by `10^(-3 D / (44100 T))` with T = Decay = 0.5 s before it
  reaches the output. After removing it both networks share one coefficient per line index to 1e-5
  (table above). The law of those eight coefficients is OPEN.
- MEASURED. The other input feeds lines 1 to 6 with 0.57233, line 7 with 0.00000 and line 8 with
  0.04215 of the same-side gain (README item 5 explained). The second pass 1->1 keeps its ratio to
  the first pass of line 1 on the cross paths (0.17941 and 0.17896, the same-side values), so the
  cross feed happens at the inputs of the lines.

### 6. Second passes inside the scored window

The scored window is not first-pass only: the second pass through line 1 lies in raw samples
2363..2532 (left) and 2381..2550 (right), and the pairs 1->2 and 2->1 of the left network reach its
last samples. MEASURED on the clean validation render with parts of the stored model switched off:
all four paths -70.08 dB; without the pairs 1->2, 2->1, 2->2 the left output falls to -32.4 and
-30.6 dB (the right output is unchanged); without any second pass -26.8 dB.

- FITTED on a window to raw 2700 (ratios to the first-pass gain of the first line; left, right):
  1->1 0.17941, 0.17896; 2->2 0.17577, 0.17510; 1->2 0.24216, 0.24126; 2->1 -0.13018, -0.12985;
  1->3 0.2592, 0.2587; 3->1 -0.1183, -0.1180.
- INFERRED from them: `ratio(a->b) = M_ba * decay_b * out_b / out_a` with |M| = 0.2478 for every pair
  measured (1->1 and 2->2 directly: 0.24781, 0.24784; products 1->2 x 2->1 and 1->3 x 3->1: 0.24778,
  0.24784), output weights out_2/out_1 = 1.378, out_3/out_1 = 1.517. Signs seen (rows = first line 1..5,
  columns = second line): `+++++ / -+-+- / --++ / +-- / --`, a Hadamard-like pattern. 0.2478 is 0.9912/4.
- FITTED. The fed-back signal passes a short kernel that the first pass does not see:
  [1, -0.0004, -0.0037, -0.0024, -0.0024, -0.0026, -0.0013, -0.0021, -0.0007, -0.0017, -0.0004, -0.0014],
  sum 0.9809. With it the last 200 raw samples improve from about -59 to -68 dB re the window and the
  whole window from -66.3 to -70.1 dB (clean validation).

## What did not hold

- Linear interpolation at the fractional part of the arrival's position in 48 kHz samples (the
  reconnaissance's test): correlation 0.005.
- A pure sinusoidal delay law at any rate (-27.8 dB at best), and the delay evaluated at the write time.
- One fixed kernel entirely in front of or behind the lines (-50 dB).
- Free least squares for the pre-kernel: the split between pre- and post-kernel is only weakly
  constrained (through the Doppler term, at most 0.33 % delay change per sample) and the alternating
  fit runs off into components near 22 kHz. The pre-kernel is therefore constructed, not fitted.
- A trajectory search with the first-pass depth for further arrivals: the extra arrival has twice the
  depth and was only found by tracking its peak.
- Macro 100 % as "the Macro 0 first pass, delayed, filtered and scaled per impulse": null -0.3 dB.

## Limits and open questions

- Scope: Macro 0, Size 100 %, Decay 0.5 s, Width 100 %, 48 kHz. The decay-gain law was only tested
  through the length differences between the two networks at one Decay setting.
- The two converters are known as their product only; equal halves are FITTED (table in 4), not proven.
  Their aliasing and imaging are not modelled: the reference has a floor of -70 to -77 dB re pass-band
  above 21.4 kHz that the model does not produce.
- What remains at -70 dB on clean captures is spread evenly over the arrivals (-66 to -70 dB re the
  window per 100 samples wherever an arrival sits) and over frequency. One component seen in the
  single-line study is a jitter of the arrival peak that correlates with neither the fraction nor the
  velocity of the delay (INFERRED: single-precision read position). Not resolved.
- The post-kernel and the loop kernel are fitted kernels, not named filters.
- Second passes: four pairs carry gains, two more were measured; the full 8 x 8 matrix, the output
  weights and the meaning of 0.2478 are OPEN, as are all later passes.
- The closed form of the phase step and of the eight line coefficients; the rule that picks the primes.

## Tide 50 % and 100 %, Mix 100 % (probe, PRELIMINARY)

Twenty left impulses 1 s apart, three instances each (`facts.checks.macro` in the data file).

- MEASURED, Macro 100 %: the undelayed Macro 0 first pass is gone (projection gain within +-0.03 on 56
  of 60 responses; the impulse at 10 s gives 0.15 to 0.21 in all three instances, where the delayed
  pattern overlaps the undelayed one). The same eight-arrival pattern comes later, and its delay grows linearly with the
  time of the impulse: 528, 533, 538, 544, 548, 554, 559, 564, 570, 575, 581, 588 host samples for
  impulses at 1..12 s, about 5.5 samples per second, identical in the three instances while they can
  be tracked (up to 17 s in one of them: 616 samples). Inside the pattern the eight arrivals shift by
  different amounts (+524 to +529 at 1 s) and each has a negative lobe two samples after its peak.
- MEASURED, Macro 100 %: the level of that delayed pattern swells (envelope gain re Macro 0 from 0.4
  to 1.2 and back to 0.3 within 20 s) and the instances differ: two of them agree at -23 to -44 dB for
  the first six seconds and are unrelated after ten; the third differs from the first impulse on.
- MEASURED, Macro 50 %: both layers at once: the undelayed Macro 0 first pass with a slowly swelling
  gain (0 to 0.46 to 0.04 over 20 s in one instance, other timing in the others) and the delayed
  pattern with the same delay ramp (544, 548, 553, ... 622).
- INFERRED: Tide adds a second layer behind a slowly sweeping delay (near 11 ms, rising 0.115 ms/s
  over the 20 s observed) with slow, instance-dependent gain; the sixteen lines and their oscillators
  stay the foundation. The sweep law beyond 20 s, the swell law and the pulse shape of the delayed
  layer need their own packets. Nothing in `first_order_b.py` models them.
