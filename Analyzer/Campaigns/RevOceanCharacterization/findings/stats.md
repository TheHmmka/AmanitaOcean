# Statistical descriptors and reference targets (packet `stats`)

Objective descriptors for judging a Tide model where a sample-level null is
impossible: the late response, and every setting with Macro above 0.

Tags: MEASURED (read off captures), FITTED (a model constant, residual given),
INFERRED (reasoned, not tested), OPEN.

## Scope: which settings were measured

- **Every capture is at Mix 100 %** (wet only). The plug-in reads the value back
  as "100" in all 37 cases (`cases.*.readback` in `targets.json`). MEASURED.
- **Macro is the Tide control**: a fresh instance is in Tide mode (README, item
  1), so the host parameter "Macro" is the Tide amount. It was measured at
  **0, 50 and 100 %** (read back "0.000", "50.0", "100").
- **Macro 100 % / Mix 100 % is the main listening point and has the largest
  ensembles**: three renders for Decay 0.5, 1, 2 s and for every Size (54 to 72
  responses per case), two for Decay 4, 8, 16 s (16 to 30 responses). Macro 0
  and 50 % are there because the task asks how each law changes with Macro and
  because Macro 0 is the foundation of the model.
- Everything else is the neutral baseline of `revocean.BASELINE`: Brightness
  0 %, filters open, Width 100 %, no pre-delay, 48 kHz, block 512, 10 s warm-up.
- Grid: Decay 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 s at Size 100 %; Size 30, 60, 100, 150, 200 %
  at Decay 2 s; each at the three Macro values. 30 impulse cases, 7 noise cases;
  171, 324 and 480 impulse responses at Macro 0, 50 and 100 %.

## Descriptors

A response is the stereo output from one impulse to the next, latency removed,
divided by the impulse amplitude. Definitions are in the docstrings; in short:

| Name | Definition | Elements |
|---|---|---|
| `edt`, `t20`, `t30` | 60 dB decay time from the line through the energy decay curve between -0.1/-10, -5/-25, -5/-35 dB; third-octave bands 63 Hz to 16 kHz filtered backwards in time, both outputs added, backward integration with truncation and tail compensation after Lundeby | 25 each, plus broadband |
| `levelTimes` | times at which the broadband decay curve reaches -0.1, -1, -3, -5 ... -60 dB; the first is a robust onset | 13 |
| `bandLevel`, `bandLevelLeft/Right`, `level*` | energy gain in dB (mean of \|H\|^2 over the band; 0 dB is the unit impulse) | 25 each, 1 each |
| `spectrum` | the same on a sixth-octave grid, 28 Hz to 20 kHz (long-term spectrum) | 58 |
| `echoDensity`, `echoDensityTime` | Abel and Huang's normalised echo density (Hann window 20 ms), 10 ms steps to 0.8 s; first time a 10 ms running mean reaches 0.9 | 80, 1 |
| `correlation`, `coherence` | real part and magnitude of the normalised cross-spectrum of the two outputs summed over the band | 25 each, plus broadband |
| `fluctuation` | standard deviation over time of the band level for periodic noise (windows of one 8192-sample period, a quarter period apart) | 25, plus broadband |
| `modulationRates` | that fluctuation split into seven octaves of modulation frequency (0.04 to 5.12 Hz) for three audio regions | 21 |
| `timeVariance` | share of band energy that is not a fixed filter of the periodic input | 25 |

The modulation stimulus is noise that repeats every 8192 samples. Its band
levels are exactly constant, so a time-invariant system returns 0 dB of
fluctuation and 0 time variance; anything else is the system moving.

Checks on synthetic signals (`test_descriptors.py`), MEASURED:

- exponential noise decays of 0.5 s cut after 0.75 s: mean T30 of 12 responses
  within 4 % from 400 Hz up and within 10 % from 63 to 315 Hz; broadband within 1 %;
- 63 Hz band, T60 0.3 s (B T = 4.4): backward filtering reads 0.302 s, forward
  filtering 0.340 s;
- noise floor 45 dB down: T30 0.9996 s for a true 1 s; the uncorrected
  integral reads 7.2 s. Response cut 42 dB down: 0.998 s against 0.981 s;
- echo density: Gaussian noise 0.997; Poisson echoes at 1000 /s 0.058, at
  20 000 /s 0.58; sparse then dense reaches 0.9 within 15 ms of the change;
- correlation: mixing coefficients 0, 0.3, 0.8 recovered within 0.01 broadband;
  a 90 degree shift gives correlation 0, coherence above 0.98;
- 10 % amplitude modulation at 0.6 Hz reads 0.605 dB (0.614 dB times the
  window's averaging); a fixed filter reads below 1e-9 dB; a delay swinging
  +-0.88 ms at 0.6 Hz gives time variance below 0.15 at 63 Hz, above 0.9 from 2 kHz.

## Ensembles

- Impulse cases: one render holds 8 to 24 impulses of amplitude 0.5 that cycle
  through the left, the right and both inputs ("L", "R", "M"). Spacing: at
  least 1.0, 1.1, 1.9, 3.6, 6.4, 9.6 s for Decay 0.5 ... 16 s, plus a
  pseudo-random extra of up to 0.25 ... 0.6 s. The slowest band has fallen by
  54 dB or more on average when the next impulse arrives, and every T30 of
  every response could be read; the reference has no noise floor (in one-off
  renders of a single impulse the tail decays steadily to below -300 dB at
  Macro 0, 50 and 100 %, MEASURED), so the cut is handled by tail compensation
  alone.
- Noise cases (Decay 0.5, 2, 8 s at Size 100 %): 40 s of periodic noise, two
  segments of 17.7 s after 4 s of build-up per render.
- Per case and descriptor the targets hold mean, standard deviation and count
  over the responses. Group "all" pools every response and holds only what does
  not depend on the driven input (decay, echo density, modulation). Levels,
  spectrum and the inter-channel relation are in groups "L", "R", "M" only:
  pooled, their spread would be the 5 dB difference between one and two driven
  inputs, not the reference's variation.
- Above Macro 0 every group also holds the standard deviation of the
  per-realisation means (`sdBetweenRealisations`).

The Decay value is not the measured decay time: the mid-band T30 is 0.97,
0.96, 0.91, 0.84, 0.73, 0.58 of Decay for 0.5 ... 16 s. MEASURED.

**Law (FITTED).** With r = 60 / T30 the decay rate of a band in dB/s,

    r(f, Decay, Size) = 60 / Decay + loss(f) * 100 / Size

The Decay knob adds a rate that is the same in every band and gives exactly
60 dB per Decay seconds; on top of it there is a loss that depends on
frequency, not on Decay, and is inversely proportional to Size.

- `loss(f)` is the mean of r - 60 / Decay over Decay 2, 4, 8, 16 s at Size
  100 %. Its standard deviation over those four values is 0.03 to 0.2 dB/s from
  315 Hz to 6.3 kHz and 0.9 dB/s (2 %) at 16 kHz: the excess does not depend on
  Decay there. Example, 1 kHz: 2.77, 2.70, 2.76, 2.74 dB/s.
- Error of the predicted T30, rms over the bands from 250 Hz up:
  **1.05 %** for Decay 2 to 16 s, **1.82 %** for Decay 0.5 and 1 s, **0.90 %**
  over the five sizes. Over all 25 bands: 2.8 %, 4.9 %, 2.1 %.
- Size exponent: fitted freely in the bands from 5 kHz up it is 0.971; with it
  the error over the sizes is 1.01 %, no better than the 0.90 % of the round
  value 1. The exponent is 1.
- INFERRED: the loss is taken once per pass round a loop whose length is
  proportional to Size, and the Decay knob sets the loop gain so that the
  frequency-independent part comes out as 60 dB per Decay seconds at every Size.
- INFERRED, not measured: for Decay above 16 s the law gives a mid-band T30 that
  saturates at 60 / loss, 27 s at 2 kHz and 1.4 s at 16 kHz for Size 100 %.

Where the law does not hold (MEASURED):

- Decay 0.5 and 1 s, high bands: the excess at 16 kHz is 51.5 and 45.8 dB/s
  against 42.7 dB/s (+21 %, +7 %). T30 is read between -5 and -35 dB, which at
  these settings is the first 0.25 to 0.5 s; the decay is slightly faster early
  than late (T20 / T30 = 0.981 and EDT / T30 = 0.83 at 16 kHz; 0.998 and 0.97
  in the mid bands).
- Bands up to 160 Hz at Decay 0.5 s: T30 is 0.576 s at 100 Hz (longer than
  Decay) and 0.397 s at 125 Hz. A third-octave band holds few arrivals there
  and its T30 is not a rate. At Decay 2 s, Size 100 %, the 125 Hz excess is
  1.8 dB/s against 3.5 to 4.4 dB/s at the other sizes, five standard errors off.

"Mid gain" is the mean over 500 Hz to 2 kHz of bandLevel - 10 log10(T30 / 13.8):
the level of the power the band's exponential starts from (13.8 = 6 ln 10).

**Law (FITTED).** For one driven input and both outputs,

    bandLevel(f) = G0 + S(f) - 10 log10(Size / 100) + 10 log10(T30(f) / 13.8)   [dB]

- G0 = 2.2 dB at Macro 0. The band energy is proportional to the band's own
  decay time: there is no make-up gain against Decay. Over Decay 1 to 16 s the
  mid gain is 2.13 dB with 0.16 dB standard deviation; at Decay 0.5 s it is
  0.9 dB lower (the 26 ms gap before the response is no longer short against
  the decay).
- Size: fitted exponent 1.031 (residual 0.09 dB); with the round value 1 the
  residual is 0.13 dB and G0 = 2.23 dB. The starting power is inversely
  proportional to Size.
- S(f), the spectrum of the starting power relative to the mid bands, in dB for
  63 Hz ... 16 kHz: -4.6, 1.6, 0.0, 1.0, 0.9, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5, 0.0, 0.4, 0.3, 0.0,
  0.0, -0.2, -0.2, -0.3, -0.3, -0.2, -0.3, -0.3, -0.4, -0.6, -1.0, -1.7, -2.3.
  Flat within 0.5 dB from 200 Hz to 6.3 kHz, -2.3 dB at 16 kHz, -4.6 dB at 63 Hz.

Other level facts, MEASURED:

- The opposite output is 8.2 dB below the same-side output (-8.0 to -8.5 dB
  for Decay 1 s and longer and all sizes; -7.4 dB at Decay 0.5 s).
- Driving both inputs gives 4.86 dB more than one input, not 3.01 dB: the
  answers to the two inputs are correlated with coefficient 0.53 at Macro 0.
  This falls to 0.26 at Macro 50 % (0.14 to 0.36) and **0.03 at Macro 100 %**
  (-0.06 to 0.15): at the main listening point the two inputs add as if they
  fed independent reverberators.

### 5. Inter-channel relation

MEASURED, Macro 0:

- From 400 Hz up the two outputs are uncorrelated: the band means stay within
  +-0.1 and the broadband correlation is 0.01 with 0.01 to 0.04 spread, for one
  driven input and for both.
- Below 200 Hz they are positively correlated. Mean over 63 to 125 Hz, one
  input: 0.86, 0.68, 0.63, 0.50, 0.44, 0.35 for Decay 0.5 ... 16 s; both
  inputs: 0.75, 0.78, 0.76, 0.67, 0.57, 0.38. The low-band correlation falls as
  the decay gets longer.
- Per band the correlation of a single response scatters by 0.03 (8 kHz) to
  0.27 (250 Hz) at Decay 2 s: only ensemble means are targets.

### 7. What Macro changes

Against Macro 0, mean over the ten impulse cases (`laws.macro*.againstMacro0`),
MEASURED:

| Hz | 80 | 250 | 1000 | 1600 | 2000 | 2500 | 4000 | 6300 | 8000 | 10000 | 12500 | 16000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| band level, Macro 50, dB | -0.3 | -1.3 | -0.8 | -0.7 | -0.5 | -0.1 | 1.6 | 4.3 | 3.1 | -0.4 | -6.7 | -16.4 |
| band level, Macro 100, dB | -4.1 | -1.1 | 0.9 | 3.0 | 3.1 | 2.5 | -0.2 | -3.9 | -6.6 | -10.2 | -15.5 | -24.2 |
| T30 ratio, Macro 100 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.02 | 1.03 | 1.01 | 1.02 | 1.02 | 1.03 | 1.06 | 1.20 |

- **Spectrum.** Macro 100 % raises 1.25 to 3.15 kHz by up to 3.1 dB and removes
  the top: -6.6 dB at 8 kHz, -24 dB at 16 kHz (-42 dB at 20 kHz in the
  sixth-octave spectrum at Decay 2 s). Macro 50 % does the same an octave and a
  half higher: +4.3 dB at 6.3 kHz, -16 dB at 16 kHz. These are means in dB of a
  spectrum that moves; one response differs from the next by 3 to 6 dB above
  2 kHz.
- **Decay time is not changed.** The mean T30 of every band up to 10 kHz is
  within 3.1 % of Macro 0 at Macro 100 % (within 1.6 % at Macro 50 %) while the
  band levels there change by up to 10 dB. At 12.5 kHz it is 5.5 % longer and at
  16 kHz, where the band is 16 to 24 dB down, 20 % longer. The mid-band T30 is
  1.827, 1.833, 1.839 s at Macro 0, 50, 100 % for Decay 2 s. The decay law of
  section 1 with the loss table of Macro 0 predicts T30 from 250 Hz to 10 kHz
  within 1.62 % rms at Macro 50 % and 2.62 % at Macro 100 % over the ten cases.
- **The decay time's spread grows with the decay length.** Standard deviation of
  T30 at 2 kHz, Macro 100 %: 3.6, 7.7, 8.7, 12.7, 13.9, 22.9 % for Decay 0.5 ...
  16 s; at Macro 0 it is 3.1 to 0.6 %. The broadband T30 spreads by 1.3 to
  11.9 % (0.1 to 0.6 % at Macro 0).
- INFERRED from the two points above: the Tide is a resonant low-pass that moves
  slowly and sits outside the feedback loop (a filter inside the loop that takes
  10 dB at 10 kHz would shorten that band's decay several times over), and it
  keeps moving while a response decays. Whether it is before or after the tank
  is OPEN; the growing T30 spread points to the output.
- **Onset.** The -0.1 dB level time is 16 to 25 ms later at Macro 100 % (4 to
  16 ms at Macro 50 %) and scatters by 4 to 22 ms from response to response
  (0.2 to 1.0 ms at Macro 0). At Decay 2 s: `16.7 ms + 0.291 ms * Size[%]` (residual
  0.65 ms) against `3.9 ms + 0.233 ms * Size[%]` at Macro 0. FITTED.
- **Echo density** builds up faster: the 0.9 time is 0.64 to 0.77 of the Macro 0
  value from Size 60 % up (136 ms against 200 ms at Size 100 %), 0.97 at Size
  30 %. `t0.9 = 12.1 ms + 1.281 ms * Size[%]`, residual 2.6 ms. A ringing
  filter also raises this measure, so denser does not have to mean more echoes.
- **Level.** One input, Decay 2 s: -9.74, -8.70, -10.51 dB at Macro 0, 50,
  100 %. The level of a single response scatters by 0.8 dB at Macro 100 %,
  1.4 dB at Macro 50 %, 0.08 dB at Macro 0. Cross-feed stays at -8.3 dB.
- **Inputs decorrelate** (section 3) and the low-band output correlation drops
  for both inputs driven (63 to 125 Hz, Decay 2 s: 0.76, 0.48, 0.37).
- The 80 Hz band is 4.1 +- 0.3 dB lower at Macro 100 % while 125 Hz is
  unchanged. Cause OPEN.

## The spread: what no model can be asked to beat

### Across realisations (Macro above 0)

`betweenRealisationsOverExpected` is the spread of the per-realisation means
divided by the spread expected if a realisation were just another stretch of
time. It is 0.44 to 0.79 at Macro 50 % and 0.44 to 1.08 at Macro 100 % over
all descriptors. MEASURED. Two instances differ by no more than two stretches
of one instance; nothing instance-specific has to be modelled. The values
below 1 mean that part of the response-to-response spread is common to all
realisations (the same impulse times meet the same deterministic 0.6 Hz phase).

## Scoring a candidate

    import json, descriptors
    targets = json.load(open("tide_structural_data/targets.json"))
    renders = {}
    for name, case in targets["cases"].items():
        stimulus = descriptors.build_stimulus(case["stimulus"])      # float32 (frames, 2)
        renders[name] = [model(stimulus, case["settings"], seed) for seed in range(case["realisations"])]
    report = descriptors.compare_to_targets(renders, targets, latency=48)   # latency=0 if compensated
    report["summary"]      # per descriptor: rmsInSpreads, maxInSpreads, rmsInErrors
    report["cases"][name][group][descriptor]   # difference, inSpreads, inErrors, spreadRatio per element

`spreadRatio` (candidate sd over target sd) catches a model that is right on
average but too steady or too restless. In a one-off check on four cases,
feeding the reference's own captures back gave 0.00 to 0.02 spreads (rounding
of the JSON), MEASURED.

## Limits and failures

1. **The impulse times of three Decay values cover a third of the 0.6 Hz
   cycle.** The spacings were meant to spread the impulses over the modulation
   cycle. They do for Decay 1, 2, 4 s (resultant length of the phases 0.03 to
   0.28). They do not for Decay 8 s (0.89: mean spacing 4.01 cycles), Decay 16 s
   (0.76: 5.95 cycles) and, per input, Decay 0.5 s (0.84 to 0.90: 0.68 cycles
   times three inputs). Consequences, MEASURED at Macro 0 with a second draw
   0.22 cycle later: band levels move by 0.46 dB rms at Decay 8 s and 1.13 dB at
   Decay 0.5 s (0.25 and 0.27 dB at Decay 1 and 2 s). In units of the stated
   spread that is 3.4 and 2.2 rms, nearly all of it below 1 kHz: 4.8 and 3.1
   spreads there against 1.1 and 0.7 from 1 kHz up (Decay 1 and 2 s: 0.3 to 1.0
   in both halves). Band correlations move by 0.09 and 0.17, per-band EDT and
   T20 at Decay 8 s by 1.9 and 1.8 spreads. T30, broadband decay, levels, level
   times and echo density stay within 0.1 to 0.9 spreads.
   So at Macro 0 the level, spectrum and correlation targets below 1 kHz of
   Decay 0.5, 8 and (not tested, INFERRED) 16 s belong to one part of the
   modulation cycle, and their spreads are several times too small. This agrees
   with the population check: the low bands are almost a pure function of the
   modulation phase. A model with the reference's modulation phase sees the same
   phases and is scored fairly; any other model should be allowed 5 spreads on
   those elements. Above Macro 0 the effect was not measured (OPEN). The fix is
   a uniform extra of 0 to 1.667 s on every spacing, which needs every impulse
   case rendered again; the cache budget of this packet is used up.
2. Modulation targets rest on 2 to 6 segments; their spread is too small by
   about 1.5 (see above). Rates below 0.056 Hz are not resolved.
3. The echo density profile first read 1 before the first arrival, because the
   previous response's tail (60 dB down, but dense) fills that window and the
   measure ignores scale. Windows more than 40 dB below the loudest and before
   it now count as silent. Kept as a property of the measure to remember.
4. Pooling levels over the three input kinds gave a "spread" of 2.4 dB that was
   only the difference between one and two driven inputs. Levels, spectrum and
   correlation are now per input only.
5. Time variance is at its ceiling from 1 kHz up and tells nothing there.
6. Not covered: other Brightness, Width, pre-delay and filter settings; sample
   rates other than 48 kHz; Decay above 16 s; Macro values other than 0, 50,
   100 %; level dependence above Macro 0 (every impulse has amplitude 0.5).
7. Macro 50 %, Decay 8 and 16 s have one render (10 and 8 responses, 3 per
   input): those two targets are the weakest.
