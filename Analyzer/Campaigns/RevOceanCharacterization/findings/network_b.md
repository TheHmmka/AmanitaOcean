## 2. The model

Internal samples `m` at 44.1 kHz, counted from the first processed sample.
Group `g` = output channel; lines `n` = 1 to 16 per group.

```
u[m]       input at the internal rate (48 kHz host: input converter of first_order; 44.1 kHz host: the input itself)
x[m + 44]  = (E * u)[m]                                        E: two peaking sections, in front of the lines only
theta_n    single-precision accumulator per line: wrap test, use, add; start 11.25 k rad (first_order)
L_n[m]     = fl32( fl32(N_n) + fl32( 38.808002 * sine(theta_n[m]) ) )          N_n = floor(P_n Size + 0.5)
r_n[m]     = (1 - f) w_n[m - i] + f w_n[m - i - 1]             i = floor(L_n[m]), f = L_n[m] - i
a_n[m]     = 10^(-3 P_n Size / (44100 Decay)) * r_n[m]         attenuation with the unrounded length (laws)
y_g[m]     = sum_n tap_n(Decay) a_n[m]                         output taps
w_j[m]     = B_j ( (1 + w_j)/2 x_g[m] + (1 - w_j)/2 x_other[m] ) + ( K * sum_n H[16 - j][n - 1] a_n / 4 )[m]
out_g      = low-pass(y_g), then 44 samples (44.1 kHz host) or the output converter (48 kHz host, section 4.2)
```

`H[r][c] = (-1)^popcount(r & c)` is the 16 x 16 Hadamard matrix in natural
(Sylvester) order, rows and columns counted from 0; line `j` is written from
row `16 - j`, so row 0 (all +) feeds line 16 and row 15 feeds line 1. `K` is
the loop kernel.
All filters are second-order sections of the audio-EQ cookbook (Bristow-
Johnson's published formulas, written out in `network_b.section`) at 44.1 kHz.
That they are these forms is measured on the fitted poles and zeros
(section 3.2), not taken from any code:

| input equaliser `E`, section 1 | peaking | 200 Hz | +0.5 dB | 0.4 |
| input equaliser `E`, section 2 | peaking | 1750 Hz | -0.5 dB | 0.4 |
| loop kernel `K`, section 1 | low shelf | 1221 Hz | -0.28 dB | 0.484 |

Laws over the lines (`S_s(x) = sign(x) (e^(s|x|) - 1) / (e^s - 1)`, the

## 3. The six questions of the packet

### 3.1 The feedback path: matrix, ordering, signs, both groups, the factor against 1/4

- MEASURED. The matrix is the Sylvester Hadamard matrix with its rows in
  reversed order, scaled by exactly 1/4, in both groups, with no exchange
  between the groups.
  - How it was found: the response of 174 impulses per group (cached 44.1 kHz
    trains of `first_order`, own input) was regressed on 16 first passes and
    all 256 second passes `i -> j` (open loop, later passes left in as noise,
    residual -14.0 dB). The 512 fitted signs are those of the reversed matrix
    without exception (256 of 256 in each group), and the magnitudes follow a
    product of one factor per source line and one per destination line within
    the 7 % rms scatter the later passes leave. The six signs of `first_order`
    are entries (written, read) = (1,1), (2,1), (1,2), (2,2), (3,1), (1,3).
  - Closed loop, regenerated by the fit script (`evidence.matrix`): the
    residual is linear in a small change of one entry, so every entry is
    fitted on it separately. All 256 entries of the left group lie within
    6.7e-6 of +-1/4 relative (rms 6.0e-7), of the right group within 1.3e-6
    (rms 3.2e-7). With any single sign flipped the null of a 3 s response
    goes from -109 / -113 dB to between -12.6 and -27.7 dB. With the rows in
    natural order the model is at +2.9 dB.
- MEASURED. The factor against 1/4 is not in the matrix. It is the loop
  kernel: a low shelf of -0.28 dB and a high shelf of -0.12 dB in series. Its
  first tap is 0.991099, so the first tap of a second pass is 0.247775
  (`first_order` measured 0.247772); at 0 Hz the kernel is 0.968278
  (-0.280 dB), at the Nyquist frequency 0.986280 (-0.120 dB). The kernel's
  taps 0.991099, -0.000120, -0.004012, -0.001879, -0.002993, ... are the
  40-tap kernel of `first_order` (0.991086, -0.000123, -0.004015, -0.001886,
  -0.002996, ...).
- MEASURED. The mixing itself is lossless (16 x 16 orthogonal). Cross-check
  with packet `stats` (INFERRED, their measurement against this model): the
  mean loop is 2931 samples, 15.05 passes per second, so the kernel alone
  costs 4.03 dB/s at 250 Hz, 2.53 at 1 kHz and 1.31 at 2 kHz; with the mean
  loss of linear interpolation (0.22 and 0.89 dB/s at 1 and 2 kHz) that is
  2.75 and 2.20 dB/s where `stats` reads 2.74 and 2.2 dB/s; at 250 Hz 4.04
  against the 3.95 dB/s of `stats_verification`. Their "loss independent of
  Decay, inversely proportional to Size" is this kernel taken once per pass.

### 3.2 Order of operations in the loop, and the loop kernel

MEASURED by the nulls of section 1 and the ablations of section 5:

1. read of the line (linear interpolation at the length of the sample being read);
2. attenuation `10^(-3 P Size / (44100 Decay))`;
3. that signal is the output tap **and** the feedback signal (the first pass
   does not see the kernel);
4. Hadamard / 4, rows reversed;
5. loop kernel (two shelves), one per line, the same for all;
6. plus the equalised, cross-fed input times `B_n`; write. No extra sample.

Kernel and matrix commute, so "kernel before or after the mixing" cannot be
told apart and does not matter. The kernel is **not** 40 free taps and not a
DC blocker: it is two cookbook shelving sections, each with real poles
(Q below 0.5). FITTED freely, then replaced by round values: low shelf
1220.996 Hz, -0.2800006 dB, Q 0.4839995; high shelf 12840.10 Hz,
-0.1200028 dB, Q 0.259996. That the sections are cookbook forms and not just
four pole/zero pairs is MEASURED on an earlier free fit of four poles and
four zeros: the cookbook ties poles and zeros of a section together, and the
two values it gives for one quantity agree to 1e-8 (low shelf) and 1.3e-7
(high shelf); for the peaking sections of the equaliser to 5e-9 and 4e-7.

### 3.5 The term proportional to the speed of the line length

MEASURED. It is the input equaliser. Packet `first_order` had one fixed filter
behind the lines; in the reference the two peaking sections of that filter sit
in front of the lines and only the low-pass behind. A filter in front of a
line whose length moves does not commute with the read: the read stretches
its impulse response by `1 - dL/dm`, which moves tap `j` by `j dL/dm`.

- With the equaliser moved behind the lines the model falls from -112.5 /
  -113.6 dB to **-68.2 / -71.4 dB**, and the error then grows through the
  response, because every further pass adds its own speed (exploration,
  Decay 8 s, before the other constants were final: -71.4 dB against
  -85.4 dB; late stretch -53.7 against -67.2 dB).
- The kernel that a filter `1, p_1, p_2, ...` in front of a line predicts in
  the excitation behind the line is `-(j p_j - (j - 1) p_(j-1))` times the
  speed. From the equaliser: 0.0163, -0.0056, -0.0126, -0.0131, -0.0112,
  -0.0087, -0.0063, -0.0043, -0.0027, -0.0014. Measured on 420 isolated
  arrivals of line 1 (regression of the excitation in front of
  `first_order`'s fixed filter on the speed): 0.0140, -0.0054, -0.0123,
  -0.0134, -0.0112, -0.0091, -0.0062, -0.0046, -0.0023, -0.0016. The first
  value shares a sample with the interpolation weight and is the least
  certain.
- The low-pass is behind the lines: with all of the fixed filter in front the
  model stood at -42 dB (exploration).

### 3.6 Size and Decay on later passes

MEASURED: they act on every pass exactly as on the first.

- Size changes the whole length of every line (`floor(P Size + 0.5)`) and the
  attenuation exponent, and nothing else: not the modulation depth, not the
  kernel, not the equaliser, not gains or taps. Nulls at Size 30, 40, 45, 50,
  62, 66, 88, 91, 149, 150, 157 and 200 % are in section 1. With the
  attenuation taken from the rounded length the Size 149 % case falls from
  -113.6 to -88.4 dB.
- Decay sets the attenuation of every pass through a line (so a path is
  attenuated by its total unrounded length, whatever its order) and the tap
  weights of the line it leaves through. The kernel does not depend on Decay:
  one set of constants holds from 0.5 to 45 s.
- The half-way cases of the rounding are covered: at Size 50 and 150 %, where
  `P Size` ends in .5 for every odd `P`, the model with `floor(x + 0.5)` nulls
  at -122.6 and -114.4 dB.

## 4. Two things found on the way

### 4.1 The line length is computed in single precision, sine included

- MEASURED. With `L = fl32(N + 38.808 sin theta)` (one rounding, the form of
  `first_order`) the model stopped at -84 dB, flat in time. The residual was
  not noise: single arrivals were off by exactly one float32 step of the
  length (6.1e-5 samples below 1024, 1.22e-4 above) and all others exact. On
  the first two samples of 2784 first arrivals (lines 1 to 8, own input)
  that form is one step off in **151 of 5568 samples** (78 down, 73 up),
  always where the unrounded value lies near a rounding boundary.
- FITTED on those 5568 samples, as a count of wrong lengths:

| expression | samples one step off |
| --- | --- |
| `fl32(N + 38.808 sin theta)`, one rounding | 151 |
| `N + fl32(D fl32(sin theta))`, `D = fl32(38.808)` = 38.807999 | 190 |
| the same with `D` = 38.808002, one float32 step higher | 106 |
| `D` = 38.808002 and a sine of period `fl32(2 pi)`: `sin(theta 2 pi / fl32(2 pi))` | 9 |
| `D` = 38.808002 and `sin(theta - k (fl32(pi/2) - pi/2))`, `k = round(theta / (pi/2))` | **5** |
| the last one with `D` one step lower / higher | 126 / 118 |
| the last one with product and sum rounded once | 55 |

  So the reference rounds the sine, the product and the sum separately; its
  depth is 38.808002, which is what `0.88f * 0.001f * 44100f` gives in single
  precision; and its sine reduces the argument with a single-precision
  `pi/2` (0.4 to 1.7e-7 rad late over the cycle). In the complete model the
  count is 5 of 5568 (`evidence.lengthExpression`).
- Effect on the null: working cases at 44.1 kHz from -95.4 to -99.6 dB
  (run 1) to -101.6 to -103.3 dB with the old constants, and to -112.5 to
  -118.2 dB after the constants were refitted on the lower floor.
- INFERRED: this is also the "3.6e-5 step below 512 samples" of
  `laws_verification` (the sum is rounded on a finer grid there; the model
  nulls at -118 to -122 dB at Size 30 to 50 %) and the "6e-7 rad lead of all
  oscillators" of `first_order_verification` (a free phase absorbing the
  depth and the sine).

### 4.2 The output converter stops one table entry early on one wing

MEASURED at a 48 kHz host. With the network at -113 dB the converters of
`first_order` left -94.8 dB, all of it in the output samples that fall exactly
on an internal sample (one in 160; -72.9 dB there, -114.4 dB elsewhere). A
free 39-tap fit of those samples nulls at -115.8 dB and shows the table
entries of the model with one difference: the tap 17 internal samples after
the output sample, which the lowered wing would read from the last table
entry (2.25e-4), is not used. With that rule
(`converter.output_low_wing_stops_early`) the same capture is at -114.45 dB
and the class at -115.63 dB (`evidence.converter`). The input converter needs
no such rule (its error would spread over all output samples; they are at
-114 dB). INFERRED: a wing whose loop ends one coefficient early is what I
recall of the classic resampler design that `first_order` names; nothing was
looked up, the rule rests on the free fit alone.

## 6. How the null falls off, and what limits it

- At a 44.1 kHz host a single impulse nulls at -109 to -116 dB in its first
  quarter second and loses about 3 dB per doubling of time: -100 dB at 4 s,
  -96 dB at 14 to 20 s, -94 dB at 20 to 30 s (section 3.4). Programme
  material nulls deeper overall (-112 to -120 dB) because its energy sits in
  tones and noise; its impulse stretches read -93 to -117 dB.
- The level relative to the reference stays there however far the tail has
  fallen: at Decay 1.3 s, Size 66 %, the model follows the reference from
  -68 dBFS at 4 s down to -476 dBFS at 12 to 14 s with a null between -91.8
  and -95.5 dB in every second (exploration). The reference has no noise
  floor of its own in this range.
- MEASURED: the largest single residuals are arrivals whose length is one
  float32 step off (the 5 in 5568 of section 4.1). On a long line a step is
  2.4e-4 to 9.8e-4 samples, which is why the worst stretch of the holdout is
  the pair of impulses at Size 157 % (-92.6 dB) and why one impulse response
  in the Decay 0.5 s train reads -85.5 dB while its neighbours read -113 to
  -123 dB.
- INFERRED: what remains is the last place of the reference's sine, which is
  not a correctly rounded sine, and the single-precision arithmetic of its
  signal path, which a double-precision model does not follow. Neither is a
  missing element.
- At 48 kHz the converters add nothing visible above that floor after
  section 4.2.

## 7. What failed, and what is not established

- First closed loops: with the 40-tap kernel of `first_order` the Decay 8 s
  response was at -64.7 dB and fell from -76 to -39 dB over 20 s (a kernel cut
  at 40 taps is wrong by 1e-4 per pass, and that adds up coherently). A free
  96-tap kernel gave -71.3 dB with an error growing as the square root of
  time; that pointed at the first-pass residual, hence at the fixed filter.
- An alternating least-squares fit of gains and tap weights converged too
  slowly to be usable (4 dB per round) and was replaced by a joint
  Gauss-Newton step. A Gauss-Newton fit of the kernel as three pole/zero
  pairs did not converge (-66 dB): nearly cancelling pairs are too badly
  conditioned. The parallel form (a gain and four one-pole terms) converged,
  and its poles and zeros turned out to be two cookbook shelves.
- Length expression: a phase lead of the oscillators (3e-7 rad: -81.9 dB), a
  constant length offset and float products with a correctly rounded sine all
  made the model worse before the single-precision `pi/2` was found.
- Converter: a regression of the residual on one table step per tap was too
  ill-conditioned to read; testing the four wing rules per lattice phase
  isolated phase 0, and a free fit of its 39 taps showed the missing one.
- OPEN: the reference's sine itself (5 lengths in 5568 still differ; a
  polynomial kernel is likely). Which polynomial is not established.
- OPEN: where `B_1 = 0.24397763` comes from. With the round ratio and shape
  the squared own and cross gains of one group sum to 0.2519996; a
  normalisation to exactly 0.252 would need `B_1 = 0.24397783`, 8e-7 above
  the fitted value, more than the 2e-7 the fit cases scatter by. Not
  confirmed.
- OPEN: why 1221 Hz, 12840 Hz, Q 0.484 and Q 0.26, and how Brightness moves
  the shelves and the equaliser (INFERRED: they are the neutral point of that
  control; only Brightness 0 % was measured). How Width moves the width
  weights. Whether tap and gain curves have a meaning beyond their numbers.
- Not covered: host rates other than 48 and 44.1 kHz, Macro above 0, input
  peaks above 0.5, Freeze.
- The scratch scripts were written against successive drafts of the model
  and are not maintained; numbers quoted from them are marked "exploration".
